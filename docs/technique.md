# The technique

The artistic and conceptual framing for the work in this repo.

## The idea in one paragraph

`slow-interpolation` is a diffusion pipeline that builds video the way an oil painter builds a season: as a small number of held moments, slowly bled into each other. A handful of prompt-defined keyframes (typically A, B, C, return-to-A) are generated through an img2img chain that lets each frame remember the last one. RIFE 64x interpolation then stretches the gaps between those keyframes into long, glacial transitions. The work refuses montage. It refuses cut. The image never arrives, it only continues to arrive.

## What makes it "slow"

Three independent mechanisms compound:

1. **Keyframe scarcity.** A 60-second loop is built from roughly 200 generated keyframes, not 1440. Most of what the eye sees is interpolation, not generation.
2. **RIFE 64x with linear timestep.** Six passes of RIFE v4.25 between every keyframe pair, producing 63 evenly-spaced intermediate frames at a linear timestep (not binary-recursive midpoints) so the motion is continuous and never accelerates through midpoints or pauses at boundaries.
3. **A meditative denoise schedule.** Each keyframe is generated by img2img from the previous one at strengths between 0.55 and 0.80, so consecutive keyframes share latent structure. The model is never asked to start over.

The combined effect is that the image drifts on the same timescale as the light coming through a studio window. The viewer does not watch the work. The viewer waits inside it.

## What the work is about

The technique began as a way to make Italian fresco paintings move at the pace of fresco (Choire v2, Casa del Suono, Parma). It then ported cleanly to Hudson River School landscapes (After Cole, submitted to CtrlShift Visions). The thread between them is the romantic-sublime tradition's relationship with slowness: weather painting, light-on-water painting, ruin painting. None of these subjects are events. They are conditions.

Generative video in 2026 mostly tries to behave like cinema, motion-graphics, or VFX. `slow-interpolation` tries to make it behave like a 19th-century oil painting reconsidered as time-based media. The contemporary question underneath: what happens when a diffusion model is allowed, even forced, to take its time?

## The Renoir flowers release (objkt labs)

The first release built on top of this consolidated toolkit is planned for the objkt labs Spring 2026 residency (curated release on Tezos). The subject will be a Renoir flowers LoRA, used inside the same pipeline.

The choice is deliberate. Impressionist floral painting is itself about slow perception: light changing on the same vase across an afternoon. Pairing it with a technique that refuses to settle gives the release a single legible thesis: a painter who tried to hold a moment, run through a system that refuses to hold any.

## Glossary

- **Keyframe.** A diffused frame at one of the discrete prompt states (A, B, C, etc.).
- **Steady segment.** A run of keyframes generated under the same prompt, with a denoise schedule that allows micro-variation but holds composition.
- **Transition segment.** A short run of keyframes that crossfade prompts (in conditioning space) and increase denoise strength so the image can travel.
- **Return.** A final transition back to A so the video loops seamlessly.
- **RIFE 64x.** Six passes of RIFE v4.25 producing 63 evenly-spaced interpolated frames between consecutive keyframes (not recursive midpoint splitting, see "Linear timestep" below).
- **Linear timestep.** Instructing RIFE to produce evenly-spaced intermediate frames in one pass, rather than recursing on midpoints (which produces a sharpness flash at t=0.5).
